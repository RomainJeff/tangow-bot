# Craft rules

Learned on the tangow "gris atténué" film over six review passes and a client who knows Apple films by heart. Each rule
cost at least one rejected cut. They hold for any product film made with this template.

## The idea

- Sell one promise, not features. Show the features the brief names, but never name them on screen.
- Every scenario is plausible and carries a customer benefit (a reply drafted overnight, a dinner plan that arrives on
  Sunday by itself). Fictional people, fictional data.
- Show the product working before the user opens it, then the pleasure of stepping in. A bot "thinking" for 17 s does
  not sell: counters start near zero.

## Edit

- Hard cuts between shots. No slow transitional moves, no pull-out to the whole phone and back in between two UI
  sequences. A shot moves a little (a 4–6° drift, a 5–10% push); the edit carries the energy.
- About 30–35 s for a reveal film. Alternate object shots (the phone in the room) and UI shots (the screen fills the
  frame); never more than ~8 s of UI in a row.
- Once the music is chosen, every cut lands on a beat (move cuts by ≤ 0.2 s) and the payoff on the downbeat of the
  track's full section.
- Time passes with the screen only: the phone at rest, the screen sleeps, it wakes on another day already working.
  The camera and the room do not change across the time cut.

## The room and the light

- One studio, one light, start to end. Changing the background or the light (day falling, a sun, a colour cast) "adds
  nothing": only the screen changes (light mode, dark mode).
- Reflections are physical only: the cover glass mirrors the room (Fresnel). Never a painted glint band sweeping the
  screen; it reads cheap.
- The opening fades up by exposure, not by changing the light.

## The phone

- It leans back like a phone on a stand (16°), hovering 1.5 cm, its base, the gap and its shadow in frame on object
  shots. Never tilted toward the viewer.
- Object shots: a tripod camera level with the floor (`ui: 0`), at about the screen centre's height. World elevation
  3–6°; in the template's phone-local terms that is `elev ≈ worldElev − 16 · cos(yaw)`, i.e. about −8 to −10.
- UI close-ups: the camera square to the glass and level with the screen (`ui: 1`), `elev: 0`, small yaw (3–6°). An
  interface that sits askew "looks wobbly".
- Three-quarter object shots show some roll from the lean; keep yaw ≤ ~35° and the camera low to keep it small.

## Words on screen

- Copy is written by a copywriter in the film's language, never translated. Short, declarative, second person.
- Benefit lines sit beside the phone on object shots only (the phone framed left, the line at 58% of the width), 2–5
  words, a two-beat line landing its second half a beat later. Never over a UI close-up.
- The opening line and the end line answer each other (tangow: "Before you open it." … "Worth opening.").

## The ending

- A last look at the phone (a slow push, the phone turning a few degrees toward the lens), then a hard cut on a strong
  beat to a flat end card: the mark, the name, the line, the availability.
- No transformation trick (the avatar stepping out of the screen and becoming the logo read cheap). Brand mark in ink on
  a light card, white on a dark one; the bots' identity colour stays inside the product.

## Review loop

- Before showing anything, a director agent (shots, lenses, framing, phone positions, numbers) and a creative-director
  agent (criteria scored /5) review the draft from a contact sheet and key frames. Apply, re-render, repeat.
- The client's taste overrides the agents: the agents praised the glint band and the step-out ending; the client
  rejected both. Brief the agents with the client's decisions as fixed constraints.
- Show the client drafts (1080p30); render the delivery master only once the cut is approved.
